# Asos

## Summary
Asos (As Seen On Screen) is a UK online fashion retailer and a holding in [[Nomad_Investment_Partnership]] by the time of Letter 23 (December 2012). Founded by Nick Robertson, it is cited by [[Nick_Sleep]] as an example of the [[Scale_Economies_Shared]] low-margin/large-customer-base model (named explicitly in Jeff Bezos's Wired interview quoted in Letter 21), and as an instance of [[Character_as_Moat]] — a founder whose motivation has moved beyond personal financial reward to the meaning and possibility of the business itself.

## Explanation

### The Bezos Model Applied
In Letter 21, Sleep quotes Bezos naming Asos explicitly among companies following the low-margin/large-customer-base strategy: "The other is to work very, very hard to be able to offer customers low margins [the Wal-Mart, Costco, AirAsia, Amazon, **Asos** model]." Asos is thus placed directly within the [[Scale_Economies_Shared]] cluster — an online retailer that prioritises customer value over margin extraction.

### Nick Robertson and Non-Monetary Motivation
Sleep's encounter with Nick Robertson, Asos's founder, is recounted in Letter 24 (June 2013) as an illustration of [[Non_Monetary_Incentives]]:

> "When we asked Nick Robertson, the founder of Asos, whose paper net worth has increased hugely since we have known him, whether, now he is a rich man, he has thoughts of leaving, his face lights up with the future possibilities of his firm and says he is having more fun now than ever before." — Letter 24, June 2013

Sleep's inference: Robertson has "moved on from monetary rewards driving his behaviour" — he derives meaning from the challenge, identity, and creative possibilities of the business. Sleep regards this as both a diagnostic (the founder is genuinely mission-driven) and a prognostic (the business will be better for it). A founder who has transcended financial motivation will make decisions that maximise long-run customer value rather than near-term personal extraction.

### Connection to Scaling Laws
Asos is referenced in the context of [[Longevity_of_Compound]] and [[Scaling_Laws]] as an internet retailer that may be simultaneously large and young — growth constrained by habit-change speed rather than by capacity or market saturation.

> ⚠️ **Snapshot — Asos discussed through Letter 24 (June 2013).** No further context appears in subsequent letters. Asos last discussed in Letter 24.
