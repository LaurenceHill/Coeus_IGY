# Georgica Plc

## Summary
Georgica Plc is a UK cue sports (snooker/billiards) business cited by [[Nick_Sleep]] in Letter 3 (December 2002) as a real-world illustration of [[Owner_Oriented_Management]] — specifically, the "cool head under pressure" quality. When the market sold Georgica shares on a misread press release, Georgica's management calmly resumed their share buyback programme the following week.

## Explanation

### The Incident
In early November 2002, Georgica issued a press release clarifying that an administrator had been appointed to **Riley Leisure Limited** — a separate company whose cue sports businesses traded under the "Riley's" brand name, which was also used by Georgica subsidiaries. The press release explicitly stated: "Riley Leisure Limited is not a member of the Georgica group."

Despite this, Georgica's share price fell more than 6% that day. Sleep's diagnosis: a nervous investor (or investors) confused the two companies, assumed Georgica had gone into administration, and sold without checking. "Nervous investors tend to shoot first and ask questions later."

### Management's Response
The following week, Georgica's management **resumed their share repurchase programme**. Sleep's view: this was exactly the right response — a signal of rational confidence in the business's intrinsic value at a moment when the share price was artificially depressed by someone else's mistake.

> "A cool head under pressure is what is required." — Letter 3, December 2002

### Why This Matters
The Georgica anecdote is not about Georgica per se — it is about the **pattern** Sleep looks for in management: the ability to act rationally when the market is irrational. Buying back shares during a mis-sold panic is value-creative for remaining shareholders. Selling, or panicking, or issuing equity at the bottom would be the opposite.

This "cool head" quality is one of Sleep's explicit [[Owner_Oriented_Management]] criteria — repeated across the letters wherever management behave counter-cyclically with their own capital (buying back bonds at distressed prices, purchasing shares when sentiment is worst, and so on).

### Nomad's Position
Georgica is mentioned as one of "our UK investments" — it was a Nomad holding at the time. No position size or valuation detail is given in Letter 3.
