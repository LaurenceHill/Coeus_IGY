# Nick Sleep

## Summary
Nick Sleep is a British investor and co-founder of the [[Nomad_Investment_Partnership]], one of the most successful long-only investment partnerships of the 2000s. He is known for deep fundamental value investing, a global mandate, extreme patience, and an unusual willingness to close a successful fund in order to pursue charitable work.

## Explanation

### Background
Sleep was a portfolio manager at [[Marathon_Asset_Management]] before co-founding Nomad. He and [[Qais_Zakaria]] (known as "Zak") acted as investment advisors to the Partnership throughout its 2001–2014 life.

### Investment Philosophy
Sleep's written voice in the Nomad letters is a master-class in Munger-style clarity: he explains investment theses in plain English, acknowledges errors openly, and consistently anchors decisions to long-run business value rather than quarterly earnings. Key tenets:

- **Absolute return focus**: Beating the index is a byproduct, not the goal — see [[Absolute_Return_Orientation]].
- **Global search for value**: No geographic constraint; the wide net is the competitive advantage.
- **Three-criteria discipline**: Value + [[Owner_Oriented_Management]] + sound capital allocation together.
- **[[Patience_as_Competitive_Advantage]]**: Sleep frequently argues the fund's edge comes as much from investor patience as from stock-picking skill.
- **Transparency about mistakes**: Openly discusses errors (e.g., Monsanto indemnification miss) rather than hiding them.

### Post-Nomad
After closing the fund in 2014, Sleep pursued charitable work, establishing the **IGY Foundation** through which the approved version of the Nomad letters was later published. He described this as the "what then" question — what to do with success beyond accumulation.

### Quotes
> "It is for this reason we named Nomad an Investment Partnership and not a fund. The relationship we seek is quite different." — Letter 2, June 2002

> "If Nomad is to have a competitive advantage over our peers this will come from the capital allocation skills of your manager and the patience of our investors." — Letter 2, June 2002
