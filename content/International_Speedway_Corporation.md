# International Speedway Corporation (ISCA)

## Summary
International Speedway Corporation (ISCA) was a US-listed company owning and operating Nascar motor racing circuits. It was one of [[Nomad_Investment_Partnership]]'s first disclosed holdings (~3.7% of fund at December 2001). Sleep viewed it as a [[Franchise_Businesses|franchise business]] with improving economics driven by a transformative media rights contract — available at an undemanding valuation because short-term investors couldn't see past the near-term cash flow lull.

## Explanation

### Business Description
ISCA owned and operated **12 motor racing circuits** in the US including Daytona, Watkins Glen, and Talladega, hosting 20 of the 39 annual Nascar races. Nascar was founded in 1948 by Bill France Snr., who deliberately designed it to be accessible, exciting, and sponsor-friendly — large fields on banked stadia accessible to middle America.

### The Investment Thesis

#### Consolidation Creates Bargaining Power
The US motorsport industry had historically been fragmented: multiple sanctioning bodies, independently owned tracks, and many media buyers. ISCA was consolidating the track side, which had a critical consequence: instead of many sellers negotiating with few buyers, ISCA could negotiate a single exclusive deal.

The result: Nascar negotiated an **exclusive seven-year media rights contract** with Fox and CBS where the rate doubled in year one and was contracted to grow at **17% per annum** through 2007.

#### Attractive Economics from Media Rights
ISCA as the dominant track operator earned the **lion's share of the 65% of revenues allocated to tracks** (Nascar kept 10%, teams received 25% as prize money). The prize money allocation was seen as a virtuous cycle: higher prizes attract new entrants → more competitive racing → larger audiences → stronger media rights at the next negotiation.

Sleep specifically notes that Fox was so encouraged by the deal that it bought a dedicated motorsport cable channel. The next contract negotiation in 2006 was seen as a potential catalyst.

#### Valuation
> ⚠️ **Snapshot — Letter 1 (December 2001).** These figures are as of Nomad's initial purchase. ISC not revisited in subsequent letters. Final state as of Letter 1.

- Shares peaked at ~US&#36;70 two years prior.
- Nomad purchased at approximately **half the peak price** (~US&#36;35).
- Market implied only **low single-digit profit growth** — pricing the business as an average firm.
- Sleep's view: ISCA was a **rock-solid franchise with improving economics**.
- The market discounted only 3–4% growth; Sleep expected substantially higher.
- Valued at "a small premium to replacement cost of the circuits" — i.e., essentially no value given to media rights or future growth.

### Why the Opportunity Existed ([[Short_Termism]])
Cash flow was cyclically subdued in the near term. Investors focused on the next quarter saw nothing exciting. Nomad's five-to-ten-year horizon was what allowed it to see value the short-term crowd dismissed.

### Risk / Uncertainty
- The France family ownership of Nascar (as the sanctioning body) concentrates power outside ISCA — they retain 10% of media revenues. Sleep notes this compares favourably with Ecclestone's near-100% take in Formula One, but the France family retains ultimate control.
- Cash flow growth projected to be "cyclically subdued in the near term."

## Contradictions / Notes
None within the first two letters. The investment thesis is stated clearly and no subsequent letters (in Letters 1–2) revise it.
