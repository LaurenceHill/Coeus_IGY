# Santa Fe Institute

## Summary
The Santa Fe Institute (SFI) is an interdisciplinary research organisation founded by Murray Gell-Mann and seven, principally Los Alamos, scientists who were frustrated with the gaps left by traditional single-discipline academia. [[Nick_Sleep]] and [[Qais_Zakaria]] became members of the SFI "some years" before Letter 12 (June 2007) — they were at that time only the second European investment firm to do so. Sleep uses the SFI's framework — particularly W. Brian Arthur's "Complex Adaptive System" model of the economy — as the theoretical anchor for both [[Probability_Based_Thinking]] and [[Scaling_Laws]], and explicitly positions it as "the new economics in waiting."

## Explanation

### Founding Mission
The SFI was founded because its founders believed traditional academia was structurally biased toward narrow specialisation: physicists study physics, not biology; this leaves many problems unsolved because they fall across disciplines. The SFI's solution was a collaborative, interdisciplinary approach to problem solving — deliberately crossing the academic boundaries that other institutions enforce.

> "Why do problems go unsolved? Because they fall across disciplines." — Letter 11, December 2006 (section heading)

### The Economy as a Complex Adaptive System
The key intellectual contribution Sleep draws on is W. Brian Arthur's model of the economy:

Traditional economists model people as rational utility-maximising agents in static equilibrium. This framework is mathematically elegant, but Arthur argues it conflicts with:
- **The biologist's view**: individuals act in self-interest, make mistakes, learn, and evolve
- **The psychologist's view**: individuals have systematic biases (what Arthur calls "little wobbles") in decision-making

Arthur borrows from all of these disciplines to produce what the SFI describes as the "Science of Complexity":

> "The real world is not just a look up table." — Murray Gell-Mann (cited Letter 12)

Arthur describes the economy as a **Complex Adaptive System** — one that evolves and learns as agents within it learn. This is the framework that underpins Sleep's conviction that behavioural finance and probability-based thinking are not peripheral refinements of economics but its successor.

### Why It Won't Become Mainstream Quickly
Sleep is characteristically clear-eyed about adoption:

> "As the saying goes, one scientist would rather borrow another's toothbrush than his ideas and... the new economics of Complexity Science is unlikely to be taught to any great degree in academia until the utility maximizing old guard... die off." — Letter 12, June 2007

This is a feature, not a bug, for investors: the longer mainstream finance ignores behavioural reality, the longer the mispricing opportunities persist for those who understand it.

### Gell-Mann on Creative Thinking
After a recent speech, Gell-Mann described his four-step model of creative thinking:
1. **Gather and saturate** — research the problem to the point of being stuck; recognise anomalies
2. **Retreat and simmer** — incubation; the unconscious mind is deployed
3. **Eureka** — whilst doing something else, the solution surfaces (Archimedes' revelation)
4. **Verify** — go and test the new insight

Sleep finds this directly applicable to long-term investing: the extended holding period is not just an opportunity for compounding — it creates the space to "retreat and simmer" between decisions. Landscape architect Charles Jencks echoes this: *"Understanding requires a certain slowing of time. Why else enter a garden?"*

### Connection to Probability-Based Thinking
The SFI framework reinforces [[Probability_Based_Thinking]] by providing a theoretical grounding for why markets systematically misprice: if the dominant model of human behaviour (rational utility maximisation) is empirically wrong, then prices set by agents using that model will be systematically wrong. Those who understand the true model — adaptive, biased, learning agents — will find reliable mispricings. The [[Equity_Yield_Curve]] is one such mispricing: the market under-compensates for the value of patience because the dominant participant has a < 1 year holding horizon.

### Connection to Scaling Laws
The SFI's interdisciplinary model enabled Geoff West to cross from physics into biology to study [[Scaling_Laws]] — work that Sleep applies directly to company analysis. Without the SFI's mandate to ignore disciplinary boundaries, this cross-domain insight would not have existed.
