# Warren Buffett

## Summary
Warren Buffett is the Chairman and CEO of Berkshire Hathaway and the most cited external reference in the [[Nomad_Investment_Partnership]] letters. Sleep and Zakaria reference Buffett as a foundational intellectual influence — his early partnership letters, his views on market behaviour, and Berkshire itself as a direct holding. Sleep wrote Buffett a personal letter upon closing Nomad in 2014.

## Explanation

### Influence on Nomad's Philosophy

#### The 1960 Buffett Partnership Letter
The most substantive early reference is Sleep's direct quotation of Buffett's **1960 partnership letter** in Letter 2 (June 2002), used to explain how a value investor's relative returns should be expected to behave:

> "I have pointed out that any superior record which we might accomplish should not be expected to be evidenced by a relatively constant advantage in performance compared to the Average. Rather it is likely that if such an advantage is achieved, it will be through better-than-average performance in stable or declining markets and average, or perhaps even poorer-than-average performance in rising markets." — Buffett, 1960

Sleep frames this as "axiomatic" and uses it to set expectations for partners: Nomad will look relatively poor in booms and relatively good in busts. This is not a flaw — it is the intended design. See [[Absolute_Return_Orientation]].

### Berkshire Hathaway as a Holding
Nomad owned Berkshire Hathaway shares for approximately **ten years** (from sometime in the early 2000s through 2014). Sleep sold the shares as part of the fund closure process and wrote Buffett a personal letter to express gratitude. He attributed Berkshire as a major contributor to Nomad's ~US&#36;2 billion total return for partners.

> "The performance that our clients have received is the capitalisation of the success of the firms in which we have invested. In other words, the real work is done by you and the good people of Berkshire." — Sleep to Buffett, June 3, 2014

This is a characteristically Munger-esque attribution of returns to the underlying business owners rather than to the investor's own skill.

### Charlie Munger
Buffett's partner at Berkshire is [[Charlie_Munger]], who is referenced independently in the Nomad letters for the "cancer surgery" mental model (Letter 3). See [[Charlie_Munger]].

### The Buffett Partnership Letters as a Model
Sleep describes receiving the early Buffett Partnership letters as fortuitous. He models Nomad's letters partly on them — the same transparency about mistakes, the same framing of relative vs. absolute returns, and the same long-form investment case writing.
