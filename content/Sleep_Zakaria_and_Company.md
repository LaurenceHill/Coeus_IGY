# Sleep, Zakaria and Company, Ltd.

## Summary
Sleep, Zakaria and Company, Limited is the investment management firm founded by [[Nick_Sleep]] and [[Qais_Zakaria]] after they resigned from [[Marathon_Asset_Management]] in March 2006. It became the sole manager of the [[Nomad_Investment_Partnership]] effective September 12, 2006 — a structural milestone that ended Nomad's dependence on Marathon's infrastructure and allowed Sleep and Zakaria to dedicate 100% of their professional time and personal investments to Nomad.

## Explanation

### Formation and Authorisation
Sleep and Zakaria resigned from Marathon in March 2006. Their new company was authorised by the Financial Services Authority (FSA) in approximately **four weeks** — a record that Sleep attributes to Nomad's operational simplicity. (The FSA queried whether the reported average of three trades per month was a typo; Sleep confirmed it was correct, noting that with an average holding period of around five years, Nomad did not trade — it invested.)

The management handover from Marathon to Sleep, Zakaria and Company was completed **September 12, 2006**, after which, in Sleep's words, "head and body will be reunited and we will assume full responsibility for your Partnership. From early September the buck stops here."

The firm was incorporated in England (Registration No. 5636487) and operated initially from Zak's home in west London ("1a, Burnsall Street, London SW3"), pending a permanent office above a shop and opposite a Chinese restaurant in west London.

### Structural Commitments
The legal structure was deliberately designed to prevent Sleep and Zakaria from selling the fund to another manager:
> "We have asked our lawyers to structure the arrangement so that Zak and I could not sell Nomad to another fund manager — that way you will not wake up with someone else managing your money whilst your manager catches a plane to Hawaii! You can sack us, but we won't sell you." — Letter 10, June 2006

This is a direct expression of the [[Principal_Agent_Conflict]] resolution: the manager's exit cannot harm the investor.

### Fee Structure
The firm introduced a fundamentally different fee model (see [[Principal_Agent_Conflict]] for detail):

**Management fee**: Cost-reimbursement basis — pays the actual cost of running the operation (salaries, rent, legal, research, etc.). Estimated 0.7–1% of NAV p.a. at inception, capped at 1%. Not a profit centre. As the Partnership grows, this fee declines as a percentage of assets, sharing scale economics with all investors.

**Performance fee**: Deferred and refundable — banked rather than paid out immediately, returned to investors in part if subsequent performance deteriorates. A high-water mark compounding at 6% p.a. means each year must genuinely earn above 6% for performance fees to accrue permanently. This structure was rejected by most fund administrators as too non-standard; the FSA by contrast found it straightforwardly reasonable.

Sleep and Zakaria accepted personal pay cuts to establish this structure, and committed to funding any management fee deficit out of pocket if costs exceeded the capped amount.

### Personal Alignment
From September 2006:
- Sleep and Zakaria dedicated **100% of their professional time** to Nomad (vs. previously "something less than 100%" while at Marathon)
- **100% of their personal investments** were in Nomad (vs. previously "something less than 100%")

### Single-Fund Mandate
> "We will manage one fund, the Nomad Investment Partnership. I expect this statement to be as true in twenty-years' time, as it is today — Zak and I are not in the product diversification business." — Letter 10, June 2006

This is a deliberate rejection of asset-gathering as a business model. The firm would not diversify into multiple strategies or build a commercial enterprise beyond Nomad.

### Fund Reopening Policy
Nomad will only reopen to new investors if incremental dollars **lower the price-to-value ratio of the Partnership as a whole**. Capital raises take the form of rights issues (existing investors first), with a queue ordered by principals-above-agents and then by qualitative criteria — not by asset size. The firm's motto: "The right crowd, and no crowding" (borrowed from the Brooklands Motor Racing Circuit).

### Relationship with Marathon
The departure was amicable. Sleep noted that Marathon's willingness to spin off Nomad was "a generous gesture" and "indicative of the friendship and out-of-the-box, non-consensual thinking that is typical of Marathon." Sleep, Zakaria, and Jeremy Hosking (Marathon's founder) attended the Berkshire Hathaway AGM together the week before the transition letter was written.
