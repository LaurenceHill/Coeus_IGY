# Black Arrow Group

## Summary
Black Arrow Group plc was a minor holding in [[Nomad_Investment_Partnership]], notable primarily as an accounting curiosity — Sleep's "dividend alchemy" example. The company was de-listed from AIM (a UK stock exchange) in February 2010, carried at nil value in the Partnership (paper loss ~0.1% of NAV), and subsequently declared a one-penny-per-share dividend. Since the holding was valued at zero, the dividend created income from a nil-value asset — money, apparently, from nothing.

## Explanation

### Background
Black Arrow Group plc was founded by — and continues to be run by — the Arnold brothers, who own or control approximately **88% of the shares outstanding**. At the time of de-listing from AIM in February 2010, book value approximated **£1 per share**, although the last traded share price was **52 pence**.

### Valuation Problem
With no market for the shares and no prospect of a market (the Arnold brothers do not wish to sell more, and Nomad tried), Sleep and Zakaria faced a valuation dilemma: the business was known to be troubled but almost certainly not worth zero. However, assigning a positive value would have been equally arbitrary since there was no mechanism to convert that value to cash. Sleep's resolution: "almost all valuations are wrong, in effect" — carry at nil and be honest about it.

> "Zak and I know the business is troubled but it is also not worth nothing; then again to assign a positive value would be just as arbitrary as we could not give you cash for your shares either - so zero it is." — Letter 21, December 2011

### The Dividend Alchemy
A one-penny-per-share dividend from a nil-valued holding produces, in accounting terms, income from nothing — Sleep's whimsical "dividend alchemy." He notes the alchemists were never this good. The dividend was declared after the de-listing, during a period when the business "continued to operate" despite its difficulties.

> "We remain hopeful, but not optimistic, that the dividend alchemy will continue." — Letter 21, December 2011

### Investment Lesson
The Black Arrow case illustrates several principles Sleep returns to across the letters:
- The inherent approximation in all valuations, especially for illiquid/unlisted holdings
- The importance of honest disclosure when investments go wrong rather than gaming the valuation
- The occasional upside surprise from a position already written to zero — a reminder that intrinsic value and book value are not always the same
