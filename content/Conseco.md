# Conseco

## Summary
Conseco was a US insurance and financial services company that filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection in December 2002. It was an acknowledged investment mistake by [[Nick_Sleep]] and [[Nomad_Investment_Partnership]]. Sleep retained the shares through the bankruptcy, writing them down to near-worthless. It is notable primarily as an example of Sleep's transparency about errors.

## Explanation

### What Happened
Conseco filed for Chapter 11 in December 2002. A full "mea culpa" was published in Nomad's September 2002 Global Investment Review (Volume 16, No. 6) before the bankruptcy filing. Sleep retained the shares in the portfolio at their nominal value rather than writing them off or hiding the mistake.

### Sleep's Stance on Mistakes
> "Investment mistakes are inevitable and indeed to some extent desirable, and we have no interest in hiding them from you (or in portfolio window dressing) — as they say, it is what it is." — Letter 3, December 2002

This is consistent with Sleep's broader philosophy: mistakes are acknowledged promptly, explained in full, and left visible in the portfolio. The contrast is with "window dressing" — the common fund manager practice of selling losing positions before a reporting date to improve the appearance of the portfolio. See [[Owner_Oriented_Management]] and the general transparency theme across the letters.

### Investment Context
Conseco was classified in the deep value workout bucket of the [[Portfolio_Classification_Framework]]. The timing (December 2002) placed it in a period when Sleep was otherwise making good decisions — the performance table shows Nomad up +1.3% for the year vs. MSCI -19.5%. The Conseco loss was absorbed without preventing decent absolute returns overall.

## Notes
- The position is written off by December 2002. In Letter 5 (December 2003), Sleep returns to Conseco briefly in the context of cognitive biases: "The Partnership's investment in Stagecoach has in effect paid for the failure of Conseco six times over, and yet it was Conseco that we wrote to you about first." See [[Cognitive_Biases_in_Investing]]. No material new investment data is provided in Letters 5–6.
