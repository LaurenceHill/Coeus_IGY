# Charlie Munger

## Summary
Charlie Munger is Vice Chairman of Berkshire Hathaway and [[Warren_Buffett]]'s long-term partner. He is referenced in the [[Nomad_Investment_Partnership]] letters as the source of the **"cancer surgery approach"** mental model for corporate turnarounds. He is also implicitly present throughout — Sleep and Zakaria's multi-disciplinary, inversion-focused, jargon-free investment style is deeply Munger-inflected.

## Explanation

### The "Cancer Surgery Approach"
The primary direct reference to Munger appears in Letter 3 (December 2002) in the discussion of [[Stagecoach_Group]]:

> "Souter began cutting away the weak businesses, a process Charlie Munger, vice Chairman of Berkshire Hathaway refers to as the 'cancer surgery approach'. This often works because there is normally a jewel at the heart of most companies that has often been used to fund new ventures or is taken for granted by impatient management. As the jewel becomes diluted by less successful projects aggregate performance declines and valuations atrophy or even fall."

The model: poorly performing subsidiary businesses accumulate around a genuinely excellent core. Management (or activist pressure) eventually cuts away the non-core — "cancer surgery" — and the jewel is revealed, causing a re-rating.

Sleep offers the canonical example: **Coca-Cola in the mid-1980s** was a poorly defined conglomerate that included a shrimp farm, winery, film studio, and even its own bottling plants. As non-core businesses were stripped out to reveal the syrup manufacturing and marketing operation, the stock rose **ten-fold** in the succeeding decade. Munger was a direct investor in this Coca-Cola recovery.

The Stagecoach thesis (Letter 3) is explicitly framed using this mental model: sell or close the US Coach USA operations and other weak businesses; return to the core UK bus business (the "jewel" which management called their "Cinderella business").

### Broader Munger Influence (Implicit)
While not always explicitly cited, Munger's fingerprints are visible throughout the Nomad letters:
- The emphasis on **inversion** (asking what can go wrong before what can go right)
- The use of **multi-disciplinary mental models** drawn from economics, psychology, and business rather than from finance theory alone
- The honest acknowledgement of **mistakes** and what was missed (the Monsanto indemnification; Conseco bankruptcy)
- The disregard for complexity and financial engineering (no leverage, no derivatives)
- The preference for **simple businesses** well run over complex businesses poorly run

## Contradictions / Notes
None within the letters reviewed to date.
