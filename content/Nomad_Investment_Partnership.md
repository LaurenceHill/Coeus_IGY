# Nomad Investment Partnership

## Summary
Nomad Investment Partnership was a long-only, global, value-oriented investment fund launched in early September 2001 by [[Nick_Sleep]] and [[Qais_Zakaria]] under the advisory firm [[Marathon_Asset_Management]] (later Sleep, Zakaria and Company, Ltd.). It ran from September 2001 to early 2014 — approximately 12.5 years across 14 calendar years — compounding at approximately 20.8% per annum before fees (18.4% after fees) over that period. It was explicitly structured as a partnership rather than a fund to signal a different, longer-term relationship with investors.

## Explanation

### Launch and Structure
- Launched **September 10, 2001** — one day before 9/11, fortuitously allowing rapid deployment into falling prices.
- Administered initially by **Daiwa Securities Trust and Banking**; later by **Phoenix Financial Services, Dublin** (which subsequently completed a management buyout from its parent, with Gavin Gray leading the new firm).
- Management fee structured as a **cost reimbursement**, starting at approximately 50–70 basis points and declining as NAV grew — reaching fewer than **10 basis points** by 2012. Performance fees were the primary incentive alignment.
- Used **no leverage, shorting, or financial derivatives** — results were achieved entirely through buying undervalued securities.
- General Partner: [[Marathon_Asset_Management]] for the early period, later Sleep, Zakaria and Company, Ltd.

### Investment Mandate
Nomad had as broad a mandate as imaginable — common shares, preferred shares, debt, or convertible bonds, across any geography. The fund was deliberately global because finding businesses that simultaneously met all three criteria was rare, and a global remit maximised the search space.

In **January–February 2003** (Letter 4), the Partners unanimously approved an expansion of the investment policy to include **unlisted equity**, enabling the [[Weetabix_Limited]] investment. Sleep credits the speed of Partners' approval for allowing purchase while prices were "particularly depressed." The fund name "Nomad" was deliberately chosen to reflect this mandate breadth — able to invest across geographies, asset classes, and listing status.

### Three Core Investment Criteria
Sleep and Zakaria looked for companies satisfying all three conditions simultaneously:
1. **Trading at approximately half of real business value** — see [[Margin_of_Safety]].
2. **Owner-oriented management** — see [[Owner_Oriented_Management]].
3. **Capital allocation consistent with long-term shareholder wealth creation**.

### Portfolio Classification Framework
Throughout the letters the portfolio was described in three buckets — see [[Portfolio_Classification_Framework]]:
- **Franchise businesses** ("difficult to copy"): media, racetracks, consumer brands, newspapers. ~37–41% of assets.
- **Asset-backed businesses**: hotels, casinos, property, conglomerates where balance sheet value dominates. ~27–31%.
- **Deep value workouts**: profitability temporarily depressed, often with debt. ~17–22%.

Note: 2006 was the only year in which Nomad underperformed the MSCI World index (+13.6% vs +20.1%). Sleep attributed this partly to the operational workload of transitioning from Marathon to [[Sleep_Zakaria_and_Company]], and partly to Nomad's companies reinvesting heavily while the market rewarded high current free cash flow.

2008 was Nomad's worst absolute year (-45.3%), but also one of its best relative performances: the MSCI World fell -40.7%. Sleep attributed the drawdown almost entirely to sentiment compression on SES businesses — Amazon's price fell far more than its revenues declined. By Letter 16 (June 2009) the partnership had recovered to +144.8% cumulative.

2009 was Nomad's strongest year: +71.5% vs +30.0% MSCI. Sleep attributed this primarily to the underlying businesses, not to his own insight. He also credited two specific partner actions: (1) the vote in early 2008 to delist from the Irish Stock Exchange — this removed artificial caps on individual position sizes, leaving NAV approximately 20% greater; (2) partners not redeeming in 2009, which Sleep estimated contributed approximately 70% of return on its own.

2013 was Nomad's second-strongest year: +62.2% vs +26.7% MSCI. By end of 2013, a dollar invested at inception was worth &#36;10.21 before fees — "just over ten dollars" — compounding at 20.8% per annum before fees / 18.4% after fees over 12+ years. Sleep's attribution: "the performance that you receive, as partners in Nomad, is the capitalisation of the success of the firms in which we have invested... The wealth you receive as partners came from the relationship our companies' employees have with their customers. All Zak and I have done is catch some better waves and try to ride them to the shore."

2011 was -9.9% for Nomad (MSCI -5.5%), but Sleep described the portfolio's return on capital as "over twice that of competing businesses" and weighted average revenue growth as "over thirty percent per annum" — the underperformance was in share price, not business performance. By mid-2012, Nomad had recovered to +23.3% YTD, pushing cumulative returns to +455.2% since inception. A dollar invested at inception in September 2001 was worth &#36;5.55 (before fees) by June 30, 2012.

Nomad made approximately **US&#36;2 billion for its clients** over its lifetime, predominantly charities and educational endowments. [[Nick_Sleep]] wrote to [[Warren_Buffett]] directly on closure to attribute a large part of that to Berkshire Hathaway.

### Closing (2014)
The fund was liquidated in early 2014 — a few months after the December 2013 annual letter — and cash returned to investors. Nick Sleep and Qais Zakaria stepped back from professional fund management to pursue charitable work and a new investing venture of their own. As Sleep wrote in the 2021 Postamble: "We wound up at an age (mid-40s) when it forced us to build something new... we would have to eat our own cooking."

### Letter 25 (December 2013) — The Final Letter

**Performance:** Full year 2013: +62.2% (MSCI +26.7%). Since inception to Dec 2013: +921.1% cumulative; &#36;10.21/dollar before fees; 20.8% p.a. before fees / 18.4% after fees.

**Key themes:**
- **Attribution of performance**: Sleep explicitly disclaim credit. "The performance that you receive, as partners in Nomad, is the capitalisation of the success of the firms in which we have invested... The wealth you receive as partners came from the relationship our companies' employees (using the company as a conduit) have with their customers." Sleep thanks the founders: Bezos (Amazon), Robertson (Asos), Fernandes (AirAsia), Sinegal (Costco), Buffett (Berkshire), Steiner, et al. "All Zak and I have done is catch some better waves and try to ride them to the shore. Riding waves is not an expensive activity and Nomad's management fee remains around ten basis points per annum."
- **[[Regulation_and_Industry_Structure]]**: Major theme. Number of regulators: four (IMRO → SFA → FSA → FCA). Scope up ~10×. New rules treat Nomad identically to a leveraged hedge fund. Sleep's preferred remedy: "If you really want to change industry behaviour, I'll whisper this, tax short term investing." Fears: industry consolidation driven by compliance burden → increased market fragility (Taleb); proliferation of intermediaries as proprietors abdicate responsibility. The halcyon days of running a partnership from "5505, Farnam Street, Omaha" are long gone.
- **Zimbabwe exit (final retrospective)**: Nomad's final Zimbabwean investments sold in 2012/2013. Despite: ~50% economic contraction; hyperinflation; currency abandonment; stock exchange suspension; forced farm nationalisations; political violence; Mugabe coalition. Returns: **3–8× in USD**. Sleep: "Not one we would repeat in a hurry (we can do better with the compounding businesses these days — and they are much less stressful)." Each limited partner received a **100 trillion Zimbabwean dollar banknote** as a souvenir. See [[Deep_Discount_to_Replacement_Cost]].
- **[[Paradox_of_Choice]]**: Barry Schwartz's framework applied to markets. 130 salad dressings; 6m hi-fi combinations; more choice → expectation of perfection → guaranteed dissatisfaction. In markets: "on any one day, month, year it is highly likely, indeed statistically almost certain, that one's chosen combination of investments will lag alternatives." Ferrari 250 GTO update: sold recently for US&#36;52m (>20% p.a. for ~50 years). "There is always something going on that makes Nomad's performance look a bit weedy!"
- **25th letter over 14 calendar years**: Sleep notes the letters have covered business models, incentive compensation, capital allocation, mistakes, psychology, and attitude — "the high level is all that matters."
- **Prospectus update**: Investment advisory agreement and prospectus being refreshed for: SEC status; US FATCA and European AIFMD requirements; Irish Stock Exchange delisting; Ralph Woodford director appointment.

**Postamble (Spring 2021)** — written by Sleep and Zakaria for the IGY Foundation published collection, not contemporaneous with the fund's operation:
- The fund was liquidated a few months after December 2013. "Selling stakes built up over years felt wrong, the clients were grace itself."
- **Why they closed**: A mixture of forces — (1) regulation was "irksome" and tools "unnecessarily blunt"; (2) wanted freedom from "justifying actions, and inactions, on an ongoing basis to a revolving door of interested parties"; (3) felt they "had wrung all that we could out of the investment process — to continue would have been to rinse and repeat"; (4) they had what they needed (a few superb businesses, unlikely to sell them to fund "another cigar butt, Philippine cement company"); (5) the pull of independence and a new adventure — recycling funds for others' benefit (philanthropy).
- Closed in their **mid-40s** — young enough to build something new, and to live long enough to see the consequences of their actions.
- William Green's book *Richer, Wiser, Happier* cited as recommended reading.
- "Good philanthropy is a minority sport for those that do minority sports. Our band could do with moving from the fringes of society to becoming the norm."
- Sleep and Zakaria had "not entirely left investing behind."

- **[[Aggregation_of_Marginal_Gains]]**: Dave Brailsford / Team GB Cycling framing applied to Nomad's portfolio companies. AirAsia's structural cost leadership is the cumulative effect of thousands of marginal decisions — not a single breakthrough.
- **Zak's family activity centre**: Real-world SES application — good costs (skilled staff, clean facilities, good coffee) vs. bad costs (newest slide); polling customers; equity-funded; founder-managed; lowering prices on achieving profitability rather than taking monopoly rents.
- **Lord Harris / Carpetright**: Founder motivation; watching the pennies (reusable price tags); motivation from academy school turnaround, not wealth.
- **School update**: Sleep's academy now in its fifth year; exam failure rate of ~85% approaching 85% pass rate. Achieved with no additional funding vs. predecessor school.
- **Phoenix Financial Services MBO**: Gavin Gray and team buying out from Phoenix Group parent. Nomad rises from ~25% of group revenues to ~75% of the new firm's revenues. Sleep and Zakaria support owner-managed administrator; hope Nomad's future performance keeps the proportion from falling.
- **"Don't just do something, stand there!"** (White Rabbit, *Alice in Wonderland*) — epigraph and organising principle of Letter 23.

**Key themes (Letter 24 — Interim, June 2013):**
- **[[Non_Monetary_Incentives]]**: Dan Ariely TED Talk (Buenos Aires, October 2012); Lego robots (Sisypheans vs. rewards group — 50%+ more robots for lower pay when meaning preserved); powdered cake mix; origami experiment; Adam Smith vs. Karl Marx (efficiency vs. meaning). - **"Nomad Inactivity App."**: Playful promise of a real-time update on portfolio inaction, "available only in the Amazon App Store."
- **Munger on inactivity**: "You make your real money sitting on your assets."

**Structural events:**
- **Irish Stock Exchange delisting** (voted by partners in early 2008, effect through 2009): removed bureaucratic cap on single-company investment sizes; estimated NAV impact: ~+20%
- Nomad explicitly "drifting toward inactivity" — portfolio turnover deliberately falling; brokerage commissions approaching single-digit basis points; Sleep likens the challenge to UK military pilots being forced to "sit on their hands"

> ⚠️ **Snapshot — updated through Letter 18 (June 2010).**

### Closure
**June 2004:** Partnership closed to new investors at just over **US&#36;100m** AUM. Sleep stated the fund would reopen only during periods of market stress when prices are depressed, not at market peaks.

**Closure criteria (Letter 5):** (1) No longer possible to invest a unit size; (2) Marketing time exceeds ~1% of working year; (3) Quality of Partner base being diluted.

### Errors / Sales (Letters 1–6)
- [[Monsanto_Company]] — sold at break-even after missing Solutia PCB indemnification
- [[Conseco]] — retained through Chapter 11; near-worthless
- Readers Digest — capital allocation mistake; made acquisitions instead of buybacks (named in Letters 5–6)
