# Marathon Asset Management

## Summary
Marathon Asset Management was the original General Partner of the [[Nomad_Investment_Partnership]] from inception in September 2001 through an unspecified early period, after which Sleep, Zakaria and Company, Ltd. took over as investment advisor. Marathon was the institutional home of [[Nick_Sleep]] and [[Qais_Zakaria]] before they established their own firm.

## Explanation

### Role in Nomad
Marathon served as General Partner — the entity with legal responsibility for the fund — while Sleep and Zakaria served as the analytical and decision-making engine. The letters reference Marathon in a handful of ways:

- Marathon insiders were described as **"the largest group of investors in Nomad"** in Letter 1, signalling strong alignment of interest.
- Marathon was a co-shareholder in [[Matichon]] (Thailand's second-largest Thai-language newspaper), where it was the third-largest shareholder alongside Nomad.
- The charts of portfolio distribution in Letter 2 credit "Marathon Asset Management" as source.

### Investment Style Consistency
Sleep and Zakaria's approach in Nomad is consistent with Marathon's well-documented long-term, capital-cycle-aware investment philosophy. The Nomad letters reflect the same emphasis on business quality, management alignment, and patience that characterises Marathon's published work (e.g., *Capital Returns*).

### Transition to Sleep, Zakaria and Company
Sleep and Zakaria resigned from Marathon in **March 2006**, after sixteen years. Sleep described the departure as amicable:
> "Between us, Zak and I have spent sixteen happy years at Marathon. It was a superb education, and in many important respects working with Jeremy has made us the investors we are today." — Letter 10, June 2006

Marathon continued as the responsible entity for Nomad's portfolio during a transition period (March–September 2006). The investment management contract formally transferred to [[Sleep_Zakaria_and_Company]] on **September 12, 2006**. Sleep, Zakaria, and Jeremy Hosking (Marathon's founder) attended the Berkshire Hathaway AGM together the week the transition letter was written — indicative of the continued personal friendship.

Marathon's willingness to spin off Nomad — allowing Sleep and Zakaria to take it independent — was described by Sleep as "a generous gesture" and "indicative of the friendship and out-of-the-box, non-consensual thinking that is typical of Marathon. Few institutions would behave so well."

The aggregation problem (Nomad's orders being combined with Marathon's main funds, causing Nomad to "move like an elephant" despite being a "mouse") was one motivation for independence. From September 2006, Nomad operated free of this structural constraint.
